About

Why I made this.

I'm a master's student in Graphic Design at ArtCenter College of Design. The Plastic Index is part of my thesis.

I care deeply about the environment. When my thesis gave me the chance to take it on through design, I knew I wanted to make a tool that pushes towards a circular economy: one where materials are kept in use or returned safely to nature, instead of thrown away.

That idea isn't new. Many Indigenous communities have long lived this way, taking only what is needed, using all of it and giving back to the land. Their way of living is what inspired this project.

There is a huge amount of research on plastic and its alternatives, but it is scattered across reports, databases and PDFs that few people have time to read. I felt that companies, and the designers who work with them, needed a faster way in. The Plastic Index turns that research into something you can explore in minutes: what is still plastic, who has already switched, what the alternatives cost and how materials can stay in the loop.

Every number on the site is sourced and dated, and estimates are marked as estimates.
